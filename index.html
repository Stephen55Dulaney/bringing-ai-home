Genesis · Bringing AI Home

AI that lives in your home —
private, grounded, and trusted.

Not a chatbot in the cloud. A companion device that lives with a person, remembers them truthfully, and stays out of the way until it's needed.

The idea

The intelligence moves into the home — onto hardware you own — instead of being rented from a data center. Quiet, local, and dependable, like an appliance you trust without thinking about it.

Running today

A companion, in a real home

A field-deployed companion device is living with a family right now — recalling the people, places, and moments that matter, grounded so it speaks only what's true. It travels with them; it powered down in one city and woke up oriented in another.

Home compute

A small home server runs local models for routine work and reaches out to frontier models only when a task genuinely needs it — keeping cost low and data at home. Local-first, cloud-when-it-counts.

We're at the beginning of the journey — but the loop is real: field feedback → a work ticket → built and verified → a portable contract → the device implements it itself → confirmed in the field.

The devices · Rose-class & Nova-class

ROSE-CLASS

The companion

A palm-sized companion built on a Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W with a voice + screen "whisper" hat: microphone, small display, push-to-talk, and a soft status light. Hands-free voice, photos of loved ones on screen, and grounded memory of the people in someone's life.

~$100 in parts · fully local · designed for warmth and trust

NOVA-CLASS

The platform body

Same hardware class, one runtime — the "body/OS" a persona rides on. Rose is a persona on Nova-class hardware. The class is the reproducible, buildable foundation; the person is what makes it a companion.

Reproducible golden image · backup-from-first-boot · persona-on-runtime

How it's different · stable in the field, self-improving in the lab

We don't ship code to devices. We ship intent — a portable contract (what to do + how we'll know it works) that the device's own agent implements for its own hardware. A device can build, repair, and extend its own capabilities.

But a companion in someone's home should be predictable. So self-improvement is a toggleable mode: devices in the field run frozen and stable — the same for everyone, low cognitive burden — while improvement happens in the lab and is pushed out only after it's verified. Self-improving where it's safe to; rock-steady where it counts.

The five pillars

Local-first

02

Companions, not chatbots

03

Grounded memory

It remembers truthfully and says "I don't know" rather than inventing — trustworthy for the people who need it most.

Capabilities travel as portable contracts; devices implement and repair themselves — with self-improvement gated for safety.

The household mesh

Devices and home compute cooperate as one system, designed to add value without adding cognitive burden.

Where it's going

From one companion in one home to the next ten — a reproducible device class, a household mesh, and a design discipline that accounts for cognitive debt: every feature must earn its keep in value, not just cost in tokens. The survival equation is simple — household utility has to stay ahead of household effort.
