Bringing AI Home · The Caregiver Companion

Rose — the companion in the room.

Not an assistant on a desk. A presence at the bedside, for the people who live there.

The room

Rose's first home is a caregiver's. A husband cares for his wife — I'll call her Susan; that isn't her real name. She has Alzheimer's. He fixes every meal, organizes every pill, and walks her back to bed when she wanders.

Rose's job is not to test what Susan has lost. Her job is to engage what she still has.

Why it works

Memory fails in an order — episodic first (what happened today), then short-term, then semantic, then spatial. Procedural, emotional, and musical memory go last. Someone who can't recall a daughter's name can still finish a song from their youth.

And the single strongest intervention in dementia care is music from ages 15–25 — up to a 67% reduction in agitation. No drug matches that effect size. So Rose isn't a quiz. She's a companion who knows Susan's people, songs, and stories, and reaches the parts of her that remain.

Rose serves the dyad — not just the patient

30–40% of caregivers develop clinical depression. The companion who sits with Susan also tracks her patterns, summarizes her days, and gives her husband the doctor-ready report nobody has time to type. In the room, for both of them.

Real today

Living in a real home

A companion deployed with the family right now — it even travels with them: powered down in one place, woke up oriented in another.

Grounded memory

It speaks only what's true and says "I don't know" rather than inventing — the guardrail that makes it safe for a vulnerable person.

Knows her people

Per-person memory of the family — source-attributed, never overwritten. New memories added by voice, or by dropping in a document.

Faces on the screen

Photos of loved ones on its display; the device recognizes who matters and brings them up.

The device

A palm-sized companion — roughly $100 in parts, fully local, sub-$200 hardware. Built on a reproducible "Nova-class" runtime: the body the persona rides on, so the next one is a build, not a miracle.

Where it goes

From one companion to the next ten. The path: reliability (hands-free voice, watchdog, endurance) → intelligence (music, photos, gentle daily routines) → depth (mood trends, caregiver respite, clinical logs) → generalize (any family sets one up in a weekend). Cost trends toward ~$20/month per home as local models take over.
